One number for a player's season, measured the same way since 1952.

Court Value (CV) rates every NBA and ABA regular season on one scale. It uses only the box-score information that exists for every year: points, assists, rebounds and shot attempts, plus how the team did in the games the player played and how well that team defended.

What CV measures

Realized value in a real season. It asks how much a player produced while he played, how much of his team's load he carried, whether his team won when he was on the floor that night, and how good that team's defense was.

Responsibility counts. A player carrying a large share of the offense gets credit for that burden even when his efficiency is imperfect.

What CV does not claim

  • It is not a plus-minus or causal impact estimate.
  • It is not wins above replacement.
  • It does not rate talent, health-adjusted ability or future value.
  • It does not settle career greatness. Peak, prime and longevity are separate questions.
  • Its defense is team defense shared by minutes, not individual defensive skill.

Methodology · version 1.0

How a season becomes a number

Every step uses information available since 1952, and every league-season is measured against itself. There are no era bonuses, no ABA penalty, and no awards or reputation in the formula.

  1. Production

    Points, assists and rebounds, with assists and rebounds at 0.65 of a point. Scoring is compared with what the league scored on the same number of shot attempts, and a mild efficiency term (0.30) rewards or penalizes the difference.

    BoxImpact = PTS + 0.65·AST + 0.65·TRB
Attempts  = FGA + 0.44·FTA
EffAdj    = 0.30 × (PTS − 0.92 × LeaguePtsPerAttempt × Attempts)

  2. Responsibility

    Players on each team are ranked by visible offensive load and share of games, then grouped into four tiers. Stars carry full weight for team context; deep reserves carry little. Tiers are responsibility, not skill.

  3. Team context

    The team's average final margin in the games the player actually played, compared with the league. Better teams earn a small, bounded boost, at most about ±12.5% for a top-tier player. Where game-by-game results can't be verified, the full-season margin is used and flagged.

    Context = 1 + 0.125 × RoleWeight × tanh(0.75 × Z_margin)

  4. Per game, within the season

    A player's season total is divided by games played, then compared with everyone in that league-season, weighted by games. Three CV points equal one standard deviation. Playing fewer games does not directly lower the score. It affects role, and a player needs 70% of team games to appear on the leaderboards.

    CV_BASE = 3 × (Rate − games-weighted season mean) / games-weighted season SD

  5. Defense

    Each team's defense is rebuilt from opponent scoring per attempt and per game. It is shared with players by minutes tier, then compressed into a bounded credit between −0.75 and +1.5. It is available for NBA 1958–2024. Other seasons are published as CV_BASE only.

    CV = CV_BASE + DefensiveCredit

Known limits

  • The box score before 1974 has no steals, blocks or turnovers, so CV uses none for any era.
  • Relative standing in a season does not prove equal absolute strength across eras or leagues.
  • Recent decades still hold slightly more of the all-time top 100 than their share of seasons.
  • A brief appearance receives the whole game's margin.

The exact specification, worked examples, validation and limitations are published with the code.
