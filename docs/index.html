One number for a player's season, measured the same way since 1952.

Court Value rates every NBA and ABA regular season on one scale. It uses only what the box score has recorded every year since 1952: points, assists, rebounds and shots. To that it adds how the team did in the games the player actually played, and how well that team defended.

Playoff CV Run is a postseason résumé on its own scale: each round counts once however long the series, and a champion's leaders earn up to 3 points of title credit by their share of the load. Playoff CV Rate is quality while active.

What it measures

Value in a real season. How much a player produced while he played, how much of the load he carried, whether his team won on the nights he played, and how well that team defended.

Carrying a team counts. A player who shoulders a big share of the offense earns credit for that burden, even when his shooting is imperfect.

What it doesn't claim

  • It isn't plus-minus or a causal impact estimate.
  • It isn't wins above replacement.
  • It doesn't rate talent, or who would have been best if healthy.
  • It doesn't settle career greatness.
  • Its defense is team defense shared by minutes, not a grade for each defender.

How it works · version 1.2

How a season becomes a number

Every step uses information recorded since 1952, and every season is measured against the other players in that same season. No era bonuses, no ABA penalty, and no awards or reputation go into the formula.

1

Count the production

Points, plus assists and rebounds at 0.65 of a point each. Scoring is compared with what the league scored on the same number of shots, and a mild efficiency term rewards or docks the difference.

BoxImpact = PTS + 0.65·AST + 0.65·TRB
Attempts  = FGA + 0.44·FTA
EffAdj    = 0.30 × (PTS − 0.92 × LgPtsPerAtt × Attempts)
2

Weigh the load

Each roster is ranked by visible offensive load and share of games, then split into four tiers. Stars carry full weight for team context and deep reserves carry little. Tiers measure responsibility, not skill.

3

Check the scoreboard

The team's average final margin in the games he actually played, compared with the league. A better team earns a small, capped boost, at most about ±12.5% for a top-tier player.

Context = 1 + 0.125 × RoleWeight × tanh(0.75 × Z_margin)
4

Compare with his season

Divide by games played, then compare with everyone in that league-season, weighted by games. Fewer games doesn't lower the score directly. It affects his role, and he needs 70% of team games to make the leaderboards.

CV_BASE = 3 × (Rate − season mean) / season SD
5

Add the defense

Each team's defense is rated by opponent scoring per shot and per game. Opponent shots weren't recorded before 1970–71, so CV estimates them the same way for every season from team totals kept since 1952. The credit is shared with players by minutes and capped between −0.75 and +1.5.

CV = CV_BASE + DefensiveCredit
6

Then the playoffs

Playoff CV is its own score, never added to the regular season. Run, the headline, counts each round once however long the series, so a sweep is worth as much as a seven-game win. Champions earn up to 3 points of title credit, shared by how much of the load each player carried. Rate measures quality while active. Minutes played are never used, because early playoff records don't have them.

Run = RunBase + Defense + TitleCredit
3

Three is the magic number

Three CV points equal one standard deviation within the season. A score of 9 sits about three standard deviations above that season's average player-game. In most years that's among the league's two or three best, whether the year is 1965 or 2015.

Known limits

  • Box scores before 1973–74 have no steals, blocks or turnovers, so CV uses none in any era.
  • Standing within a season doesn't prove equal strength across eras or leagues.
  • Recent decades still hold a few more of the all-time top 100 than their share of seasons.
  • A one-minute appearance gets the whole game's margin.
  • Estimated opponent shots miss for about one team in nine, so its defensive credit can be off.

The exact specification, worked examples, validation and limitations are published with the code.
